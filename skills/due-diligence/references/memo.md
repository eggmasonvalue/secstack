# Memo

A full memo is a decision document whose evidence a reader can audit. The spine below is the
default order. Merge, drop, or add sections whenever the company's economics call for it: a
special situation, a cyclical, and a compounder should not read alike.

```markdown
# [Company] ([Ticker / class]) — investment memo
**As of:** [date] · **Question:** [decision] · **Decision:** Long / Watch / Pass
**Conviction:** [level, and the weakest load-bearing link]
```

1. **Decision snapshot.** Give the few figures that orient *this* case (not a stock header),
   with dates and units. Then: what the price requires, the variant view if there is one, the
   decisive evidence, what would make you wrong, and why the price does or does not pay for
   that. With no variant view, say so.
2. **Business and economics.** Who pays, for what unit, and where gross profit and cash arise;
   the few variables that govern value.
3. **Reported to normalized.** The bridge that feeds the valuation, plus any driver bridge for a
   new stream.
4. **Competitive position.** The profit pool, economic peers, and two or three advantage chains
   with erosion tests. Include it only if it changes an assumption, probability, or monitorable.
5. **Stewards.** Include when control, incentives, dilution, or capital allocation can change the
   outcome. Keep lists of immaterial holders out.
6. **Valuation.** Methods, bridge, scenarios named by mechanism, dominant assumptions,
   sensitivity, and value per share against price.
7. **Countercase and pre-mortem.** The strongest case against owning it; the assumption most
   likely wrong; the permanent-impairment path; financing or control events; which downside
   figure is anchored and which is not.
8. **Catalysts, falsifiers, monitorables.** For each: the mechanism, time window, source to
   check, and the result that confirms or weakens the thesis. "Re-rating" is an outcome, not a
   catalyst. A falsifier means exit or rework, whatever the price does.
9. **Verdict.** Long, Watch, or Pass, tied to valuation and the countercase; the price or
   evidence that would change it; the work still open.
10. **Sources and calculations.** Only the sources the argument relies on: form, filing date,
    accession, and section; document titles and dates; URLs with access dates; calculation
    inputs and formulas. Preserve conflicting evidence.
