# Ownership, compensation, and governance

Each form answers a different question. Keep dates, share classes, and reporting entities
attached to every figure, and reconcile them before combining sources.

## Form 4: what insiders did

- Read the code, shares, price, direct or indirect ownership, footnotes, the derivative table,
  the 10b5-1 box, and both dates together.
- `P` is the clean voluntary cash signal. `S` needs context (plan, size relative to holdings,
  history) before it says anything about motive. `M` (exercise), `F` (tax withholding), `A`
  (grant), and `G` (gift) are mechanics; keep them out of any buy/sell ratio.
- Size a purchase against the insider's prior holdings and pay rather than against a dollar
  threshold. Clusters matter when the buyers decided independently.
- Use the 10b5-1 checkbox and adoption date where reported. A regular cadence or timing near an
  event is a question to ask, not evidence of a plan or of intent.

## 13F: what managers reported holding

A 13F is a quarter-end snapshot of long positions in reportable securities, filed up to 45 days
later. It omits shorts, rationale, small managers, non-reportable securities, and some
confidential positions.

- "Who owns this stock?" means "which 13F managers disclosed this CUSIP?" Reconcile share
  classes and CUSIPs before you aggregate.
- Ownership % = 13F shares ÷ shares outstanding *at the same date*, tagged `[E]`. A summed 13F
  table is never total institutional or float ownership.
- A quarter-over-quarter change tells you neither when nor why a position moved. A famous
  manager's name validates nothing; the position may be hedged, passive, or tiny.

## 13D / 13G: who can influence control

- Record the reporting person, group, class, shares, percentage, voting and dispositive power,
  and the event date.
- Follow each reporting person's amendment chain. Every accession is not a new holder.
- 13D Item 4 states purpose and plans; read it before calling anything activism. 13G covers
  qualified institutions and exempt investors as well as passive ones, so read the filing
  category.

## Proxy (DEF 14A): beneficial ownership, pay, and governance

The proxy is free-form: fetch it whole, read its table of contents, then grep for the headings
it actually uses. Typical language:

- ownership: "Security Ownership of Certain Beneficial Owners and Management", "Principal
  Shareholders", "Beneficial Ownership";
- pay: "Compensation Discussion and Analysis", "Summary Compensation Table", "Pay Versus
  Performance", "Potential Payments upon Termination or Change in Control";
- conflicts and dilution: "Transactions with Related Persons", "Equity Compensation Plan
  Information", auditor fees.

The proxy ownership table is the authoritative measure of insider and 5% ownership, with its
own measurement date. Reported total pay is neither realized pay nor current equity value. For
equity plans, keep authorized, outstanding, and remaining shares separate. Final vote counts
are in 8-K Item 5.07, not the proxy. Warrants, converts, and ATM programs are in 10-K/10-Q
footnotes, 8-K exhibits, S-3, or 424B filings.

## Foreign private issuers

Foreign private issuers generally file no DEF 14A and no Form 3/4/5. Use 20-F Item 6
(directors, compensation, board practices, share ownership) and Item 7.A (major holders). When
slicing Item 7 by text, end at "Item 8": the words "Related Party Transactions" also appear in
Item 7's own title. Check the home regulator when insider dealing matters.
